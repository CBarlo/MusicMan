PART 1

What Is This?

System Overview

MusicMan is a show controller that runs on a Raspberry Pi. It lets one person — from an iPad — control music, lighting, sound effects, timers, games, and the HDMI display during a live campfire show. Everyone else sees a polished production; the operator sees a simple web console.

The Pi creates its own WiFi network called MusicMan. Connect your iPad to that network and open a browser — no app to install, no configuration required. The whole show runs from there.

What It Controls

SystemWhat MusicMan does
MusicPlays walk-up music for each circle and role, background music, USB playlists, and countdown tracks
Sound EffectsTriggers one-shot SFX from a library of audio files
LightingSends scene commands to WLED LED controllers over the network; crowd lighting tracks the hype meter
HDMI DisplayShows countdown timers, leaderboards, images, animations, crowd gauge, and live slides on a projector or TV
TimersSkit timer with presets (and HDMI warning); games countdown timer; stopwatch
Show FlowA scripted sequence of steps that fire macros automatically, in order
GamesCountdown timer, stopwatch, leaderboard shown live on HDMI; Shell Game interactive display
Crowd NoiseLive animated hype gauge on the HDMI — operator drags a slider, gauge reacts, lights follow
BatteryMonitors Anker SOLIX C300 power stations over Bluetooth — shows charge level, controls AC/DC output

Hardware You Need

  • Raspberry Pi 4B — the brain of the system (already set up)
  • Power supply for the Pi (USB-C brick)
  • Speakers / PA system connected to the audio output on the box
  • HDMI display, TV, or projector connected to the Pi's HDMI port
  • USB drive with your music, SFX, and image files
  • iPad or any device with a browser — connects to MusicMan WiFi
  • WLED lighting controllers (optional — needed for lighting scenes and crowd lights)
  • Anker SOLIX C300 power stations (optional — for battery-powered pole lighting)
PART 2

Setup

Physical Connections

  1. Connect your speakers or PA system to the audio output jack on the box.
  2. Connect the HDMI display or projector to the Pi's HDMI port. Do this before powering on.
  3. Plug in your USB drive (with music/SFX files) into a USB port on the Pi.
  4. Connect the Pi to power. Give it about 60 seconds to fully boot.

The HDMI Display

This is automatic. You don't need to do anything. When the box powers on, it launches the audience display fullscreen on its own. Plug in the HDMI cable before you power on and it takes care of the rest.

If the display isn't showing
Make sure the HDMI cable was plugged in before powering on. If the screen is blank after 60 seconds, give it another 30. If it still doesn't appear, the display page is available on any browser at http://musicman.local/display — press F11 to go fullscreen. It shows the exact same content, synchronized live.

Connecting to MusicMan WiFi

The Pi creates its own WiFi network. Connect your iPad (or any control device) to it:

FieldValue
Network name (SSID)MusicMan
PasswordBrokenArrow
At home vs. at the campsite
At a campsite, MusicMan is the only network — connect to it and everything works. At home, you can also connect via your home WiFi network. The Pi is visible as musicman.local from any device on the same home network. Either way, the URL is http://musicman.local.
Tip — multiple devices
You can have the console open on multiple devices at once. Changes on one appear on all of them instantly. Useful if you want a helper watching a tablet while you run from the iPad.

Opening the Console

Once on MusicMan WiFi, open any browser and go to:

http://musicman.local

This is the operator console. Bookmark it.

For admin configuration (circles, macros, show flow, etc.), go to:

http://musicman.local/admin

Step 0: Load Your Media Files First

Before configuring walk-ups, macros, or timers, get your audio, image, and video files onto the system. Everything else references files by name — if a file isn't loaded, it won't appear in any dropdown.

Sound Effects (SFX)

One-shot sounds: air horns, drum hits, cheers, explosion sounds, buzzers.

Supported formats: MP3, WAV, FLAC, OGG, M4A, AAC, OPUS

  1. In Admin, click ♪ Audio.
  2. Under SFX, click the dashed upload zone or drag files onto it.
  3. Use ▶ to preview, pencil to rename, trash to delete.

Background Music (Music Library)

Songs, ambient tracks, and walk-up music.

  1. In Admin, click ♪ Audio.
  2. Under Music (below SFX), click the upload zone or drag tracks in.
Audio normalization
The Audio tab has a NORMALIZE NEW button. This processes un-normalized files to a consistent volume level (EBU R128, -14 LUFS) so walk-ups and skits don't jump in level between tracks. Run it after adding new files.

Display Images and Videos

Images and videos shown fullscreen on the HDMI: logos, title cards, animations, backgrounds.

Images: JPG, PNG, GIF (animated works), WebP — 1920×1080 recommended.

Videos: MP4 (H.264), WebM — MP4/H.264 plays most reliably on the Pi.

  1. In Admin, click 🖼 Display.
  2. Upload images and videos with the upload zone — it accepts both.
Large files
Files over 200 MB take a while over WiFi. For large video files, copy them directly via the Samba share or SFTP — much faster than the browser uploader.

  3. Click the card to open the editor on the right.

DMX Fixtures — static values

Each pole node's fixtures (pinspot and wash bar) have named channels defined in the Fixture Profiles (Admin → Settings → Lighting). In the scene editor, set a value for each channel you want to control. Channels left blank are not sent.

DMX Animation

Enable the DMX ANIMATION toggle to make fixture values cycle through a sequence of keyframes instead of holding a single static state.

SettingWhat it does
Interval (ms)How long the transition from one keyframe to the next takes, in milliseconds. 1000 = 1-second fade. 40 = near-instant cut.
FramesEach frame is a snapshot of channel values across one or more pole nodes. The animation loops through all frames continuously.
+ ADD FRAMEAdds a new keyframe at the end of the sequence.

The Pi interpolates channel values smoothly between keyframes at ~25 steps per second, so you only need to define the start and end states — not every intermediate value. A two-frame animation (e.g., red→blue) will fade continuously: red → blue → red → blue…

Single frame = static DMX, held on activate
If you define exactly one frame and leave animation off, those channel values are sent once when the scene activates and held until another scene fires. Enable animation with one frame only if you want to lock the values in a loop (useful if another system might try to override the fixture).
No lighting? No problem.
If you're not using WLED lighting or pole nodes, ignore the Scenes section entirely. Scenes assigned to walk-ups or macros silently do nothing if no devices are reachable.

Macros

A Macro is an ordered list of steps that execute in sequence when triggered. One tap can play music, set lights, show an image, load a timer preset, push a slide, and more — all automated.

Creating a macro

  1. In Admin, click ⚡ Macros.
  2. Click + NEW MACRO. Give it a name and optional description.
  3. Under STEPS, pick a step type from the dropdown and click + ADD STEP.
  4. Configure each step's fields. Use ▲ / ▼ to reorder steps.
  5. Click ✓ SAVE MACRO.
  6. Click ▶ TEST to fire it immediately for testing.
  7. Click ⧉ DUPLICATE to copy a macro as a starting point for a similar one.

Show Flow

The Show Flow is the master rundown — an ordered list of named items, each linked to a macro. During the show, you tap items one by one, and each fires its macro automatically.

Building the show flow

  1. In Admin, click ▶ Show Flow.
  2. Click + ADD STEP.
  3. In the picker panel, fill in: Macro (which macro to fire), Display Name (label on the console button), Notes (operator reminder — not shown to audience), and Color (button color for scanning quickly).
  4. Click ADD TO SHOW. Repeat for each show segment.
  5. Drag items in the left list to reorder. Click SAVE SHOW FLOW when done.
Always save the Show Flow
The Show Flow requires you to click SAVE SHOW FLOW before your order is persisted. If you reorder items and close the browser without saving, the order reverts.

Skit Timer & Presets

Configure the skit timer in Admin → ⏱ Timer. The skit timer is the countdown used during skits and ceremonies — separate from the Games countdown timer.

Timer settings

SettingWhat it does
DurationTimer length in seconds (e.g., 180 = 3 minutes)
Warning AtSeconds remaining when the 15-second style warning triggers on the HDMI screen
Start SoundMusic track or SFX that plays when the timer starts
Start At (sec)Jump into the start sound at this many seconds into the track
Sound DurationHow long to play the start sound (0 = full file duration)
Loop Start SoundCheck this to loop the start sound continuously and fade it out near the end
Fade (sec)When Loop is on: how many seconds before zero to fade out the start sound. When Loop is off: ignored.

Loop vs. play-once — the key rule
Loop unchecked: plays the start sound once from beginning to end, then stops.
Loop checked: loops the sound continuously, then fades it out that many seconds before zero — perfect for background skit music that fills the whole timer.

Example: Duration = 180, Loop checked, Fade = 5 → music loops for 175 seconds, fades over 5 seconds as the End Sound fires.

Saving and loading timer presets

A Timer Preset saves all the current timer settings under a name so a macro can load the right timer config for each segment automatically.

  1. Configure all timer fields for a particular segment.
  2. Scroll down to Timer Presets. Check the Will save: summary line — it shows exactly what will be saved.
  3. Type a name (e.g., "Skit Timer", "2 Minute Timer") and click SAVE AS PRESET.
  4. To use in a show: add a ⚙ Load Timer Preset step to a macro and pick the preset name. The macro will load the right timer config before the skit starts — operator just presses START.
  5. To delete a preset: click the trash icon next to it in the presets list.

Lighting Settings — Pole Nodes & Fixture Profiles

Admin → Settings → ✦ Lighting is where you configure the physical pole hardware. Everything here is one-time setup — do it before building scenes.

Pole Nodes

A Pole Node is the ESP32 controller on each pole. It runs custom WLED firmware that drives both the WS2812B LED strip (via WLED) and the DMX fixtures (pinspot + wash bar) over a MAX485 interface. Each node is controlled by the Pi over WiFi.

  1. Click + ADD NODE.
  2. Enter a unique ID (e.g., pole_a), a Label, and the node's IP or hostname (e.g., pole-a.local).
  3. Click + FIXTURE to add each DMX fixture on that pole. Give each fixture a name, its DMX start channel, and its channel count.
  4. Use the same fixture IDs (e.g., pinspot, wash) on every pole — this lets them share one Fixture Profile definition.
  5. Click ✓ SAVE NODES.
  6. Click ↗ WLED UI to open that node's WLED web interface directly — useful for testing strip effects and checking firmware status.
Fixture fieldWhat it does
IDIdentifier used in scene DMX blocks. Must match across poles for shared profiles to work (e.g., pinspot).
Start channelThe DMX address of the fixture's first channel (1-based). Pinspot = 1, Wash Bar = 7 in our setup.
Channel countNumber of DMX channels this fixture uses. Pinspot = 6, Wash Bar = 8.

Fixture Profiles

A Fixture Profile names each channel of a fixture type and defines quick-pick preset values — shown in the scene editor and the console test panel. Profiles are defined once and shared across all poles that use the same fixture ID.

  1. After saving Pole Nodes, the Fixture Profiles section populates automatically — one block per unique fixture ID.
  2. Click a channel row to name it (e.g., "MODE", "RED", "DIMMER").
  3. In the Presets column, enter Label=Value pairs separated by commas (e.g., Manual=255, Strobe=187, Off=0). These appear as quick buttons in the scene editor.
  4. Click the ≡ icon to paste raw text from a fixture manual — the parser extracts channel ranges automatically.
  5. Click ✓ SAVE PROFILES when done.

WLED LED Segments

Each pole node's LED strip (300 × WS2812B) is divided into named segments configured in WLED. Segments let scenes address different parts of the strip independently.

SegmentLEDsWhat it covers
Stage 10 – 59First stage-facing bar
Stage 260 – 119Second stage-facing bar
Audience L120 – 179Left audience-facing bar
Audience C180 – 239Centre audience-facing bar
Audience R240 – 299Right audience-facing bar
Audience All120 – 299All three audience bars as one — use this for continuous chase/rainbow effects across the full audience face

Segments are saved as WLED preset 1 and loaded automatically on every boot. They survive power cycles. They do not survive a firmware reflash — if you ever reflash a node, re-upload presets.json to restore them.

WLED Sync (Pole A → Pole B)

Pole A is configured as the sync sender; Pole B is the sync receiver. When the Pi fires a WLED preset or effect to Pole A, Pole A instantly broadcasts a UDP packet and Pole B mirrors it — both strips fire in perfect sync from a single command.

The poles use sync group 4 (not the default group 1), so other WLED devices on the network are not affected.

DMX is not synced
WLED sync only covers the LED strip. DMX fixture commands (pinspot and wash bar) are sent to each pole independently by the Pi. Both poles always receive their own DMX command.

Running the Show Flow

  1. Open the SHOW tab.
  2. Tap the first show item. Its macro fires immediately.
  3. When ready to move on, tap the next item.
  4. The active item is highlighted. Previous items are dimmed to show progress.
Tip — macros run instantly
There's no confirmation prompt. Tapping a show flow item fires the macro right away. Tap deliberately.

Walk-Up Music

  1. Go to the WALKUPS tab (circles) or ROLES tab.
  2. Tap the circle or role button. Their walk-up track starts, their logo appears on HDMI, and their lighting scene fires.
  3. The walk-up plays through its configured duration and fades automatically. To stop early: FADE & END or STOP.

Playing Music from the Library

  1. Go to the MUSIC tab.
  2. Browse or search for a track. Tap it to play immediately.
  3. Use transport controls to pause, fade, or stop.

Sound Effects

  1. Go to the SFX tab.
  2. Tap any button to trigger that sound effect.
  3. SFX play on a separate channel — they don't interrupt or pause music.
  4. Adjust SFX volume with the bottom slider in the left panel.

Lighting Controls

Lighting scenes appear as buttons in the left panel under Lighting Scenes and in the LIGHTS tab. The active scene is highlighted.

  • Tap any scene button to activate it immediately.
  • Use the DIM slider to dim or brighten all lights at once.
  • Scenes are also triggered automatically by macros and walk-ups.

The Skit Timer

The skit timer lives in the left panel. Its label shows the name of the currently loaded timer preset — or SKIT TIMER if none is loaded.

ControlWhat it does
▶ STARTStarts the countdown. Start sound plays if configured.
⏸ PAUSEPauses the countdown (fades start sound if looping)
SETShows the timer on the HDMI display without starting it
↺ RESETStops the countdown, resets to full duration, clears timer from HDMI
SHOW ON SCREENWhen on: timer appears on HDMI from the moment it starts. When off: only appears at the 15-second warning.
The 15-second warning always shows
Regardless of the SHOW ON SCREEN setting, a large countdown always appears on the HDMI display when 15 seconds remain. This is intentional — always visible to the audience, letting the full timer stay hidden until then.

Loading a preset from the console

The timer panel has a preset dropdown. Select a preset and click LOAD to apply it — the timer label, duration, and all sounds update immediately. Or use a macro with a Load Timer Preset step to load the right preset automatically when a show item fires.

Games Tab

Games Countdown Timer

A separate countdown timer for games — independent of the Skit Timer.

  1. Go to the GAMES/TIMERS tab.
  2. Set the duration (minutes and seconds) and click SET.
  3. Configure start and end sounds. Use the same Loop and Fade logic as the Skit Timer.
  4. Check Display to show the countdown on HDMI. Check End Anim to show a time's-up animation.
  5. Click ▶ START. The left panel label changes to GAMES TIMER and mirrors the countdown.

Stopwatch

Below the countdown timer. ▶ START to start, ■ STOP to stop. Shows elapsed time in M:SS.T format. Check Show on Screen to display it on HDMI. Use it to time individual game runs.

Leaderboard

  1. Create a game with + NEW.
  2. After each run, enter the participant's name and click ⏱ RECORD to save the stopwatch time.
  3. Click ▲ FASTEST FIRST / ▼ MOST RECENT to sort. The HDMI leaderboard updates automatically.
  4. Click 📺 SHOW SCORES to display the leaderboard on HDMI.
  5. Click ✕ CLEAR to clear times for the current game.

HDMI Display Controls

The DISPLAY tab controls what the audience sees on screen.

  • Tap any image thumbnail to display it fullscreen on the HDMI.
  • Tap a video thumbnail to play it fullscreen (loops until cleared).
  • Use CLEAR PROJECTOR to return to standby/dark.

Live Slide Builder

At the top of the DISPLAY tab. Use it for last-minute announcements without going to the admin panel.

  1. Go to DISPLAY.
  2. Under LIVE SLIDE, tap a template: BIG TITLE, BULLETIN, NEXT EVENT, SPOTLIGHT, or QR CODE.
  3. Fill in the fields. Pick an accent color.
  4. Tap ▶ SEND TO DISPLAY. The slide goes live immediately.
  5. Tap ✕ CLEAR to remove it.
  6. To save for reuse: type a name and tap SAVE. It appears in the saved slides list below.
Slides layer over everything
A custom slide appears on top of any image or animation already showing. Clearing the slide reveals whatever was underneath. CLEAR DISPLAY (transport bar) removes everything, slides included.

Crowd Noise — Live Operation

Crowd noise controls are in the DISPLAY tab under CROWD CONTROL. Tap 📢 SHOW VIZ to bring the gauge up on HDMI.

Manual vs. Auto mode

ModeHow it works
ManualOperator drags a slider to set the crowd level. Gauge and lights update on release.
Auto (Mic)Level is driven automatically by the microphone input from a pole node. The dB meter bars show the live input level.

The slider (Manual mode)

Drag the slider to set the crowd level (0–100%). The gauge needle and crowd lighting update on release — the needle ramps naturally, like a real VU meter. The gauge stays at whatever level you set until you move it again.

Text overlays

Below the slider is a list of uploaded overlay videos (GET LOUD, LOUDER, LET'S GOOO, etc.). Tap one to play it looping over the gauge. Tap another to switch — it crossfades without a flash. Tap ✕ STOP OVERLAY to clear and show just the gauge.

CLIMAX

  1. Tap ⚡ CLIMAX.
  2. The gauge needle slams instantly to maximum.
  3. Any text overlay stops immediately.
  4. The explosion video fires fullscreen — in sync with the explosion SFX.
  5. When the video ends, the gauge hard-cuts to black. The crowd viz closes.
Climax is one-way
After Climax fires, the crowd viz closes automatically. To use it again, tap 📢 SHOW VIZ and bring the slider back up from zero.

Dismissing the gauge

Tap ✕ (or STOP VIZ) to fade the gauge off HDMI and reset the slider and lights to zero.

Shell Game — Live Operation

The Shell Game puts a full-screen animated campfire scene on the HDMI — three tents shuffling around a hidden item. The audience guesses which tent it's under.

Launch it from the GAMES/TIMERS tab → Shell Game section, or from a macro with a 🎮 Shell Game step. The HDMI display switches to the shell game page automatically.

ControlWhat it does
OPEN ON DISPLAYNavigates the HDMI kiosk to the shell game page
START GAMEBegins the shuffle sequence with sounds
RESET TO LOBBYResets for another round, returns to the lobby screen

When done, the HDMI returns to the normal display page automatically.

USB Playlist Tab

Play music folders directly from a USB drive — no configuration needed. Great for pre-show and post-show background music.

  1. Plug a USB drive into the Pi.
  2. Go to the MUSIC tab. In the USB Playlist section, tap ↺ SCAN. All folders appear in the list.
  3. Tap any folder to play all tracks in it as a playlist.
  4. Toggle 🔀 SHUFFLE before selecting to shuffle the order.
  5. Use ⏭ NEXT TRACK to skip ahead.

Emergency Stop — Kill All

Kill All
KILL ALL is the big red emergency button. Press it if something goes badly wrong. It stops all audio, resets the skit timer, clears the display, and turns off active lights. It does not reset the show flow position — you can pick back up exactly where you were.
PART 5

Quick Reference

Pre-Show Checklist

  • Pi is powered on and fully booted (~60 seconds after power-up)
  • USB drive is plugged in
  • Speakers are connected and working — test with any SFX button
  • HDMI display is connected and showing the display page fullscreen
  • iPad is connected to MusicMan WiFi and the console loads at http://musicman.local
  • Show Flow is in the correct order — check Admin → Show Flow
  • Timer presets are correct — fire a test macro and confirm the timer label updates and the duration is right
  • Volume levels set — Music slider at starting level, SFX slider set
  • Lighting scenes working — tap one and confirm lights respond
  • Battery stations checked — Admin → System → Battery shows SOLIX state

Common Workflows

Announcing a Circle

Troubleshooting

ProblemFix
Console won't load in browser Check that your device is connected to MusicMan WiFi. Verify the Pi is powered on and fully booted. Try http://musicman.local directly.
No audio from speakers Check Music volume slider isn't at 0. Try an SFX — if that works but music doesn't, the music file may be missing. If nothing plays at all, check speaker connections to the Audio HAT.
HDMI display shows nothing Make sure the HDMI cable was plugged in before the Pi powered on. Try refreshing the display browser. Click CLEAR DISPLAY then show an image to test.
Timer label still says "SKIT TIMER" after firing a macro The macro's Load Timer Preset step may point to a preset that doesn't exist. Check Admin → Macros and verify the preset name matches exactly what's in Admin → Timer → Presets.
Walk-up music not playing Check that the audio file assigned to that circle still exists. Go to Admin → Circles and re-select or re-upload the walk-up music track.
Lighting scenes not working Verify WLED devices are powered on and connected to MusicMan WiFi. Check that the Zone IDs in the scene match the device IDs in your WLED device list.
Shell Game won't open on the display Tap OPEN ON DISPLAY first, then START GAME. The display browser must be on the shell game page before the game can start.
Something is badly wrong Press KILL ALL. It stops all audio, resets the timer, and clears the display. The show flow remembers where you were — pick up from the next step.
Changes in Admin aren't taking effect Hard-refresh the admin page: Cmd+Shift+R (Mac) or Ctrl+Shift+R (Windows).
Pi is unresponsive Try Admin → System → ⚠ RESTART SERVICE first. If that fails, wait 60 seconds and reload. Last resort: power off — but always use 🔌 SHUTDOWN PI when you can to avoid SD card corruption.
The Golden Rule
When in doubt: KILL ALL, then reload. The show flow remembers where you were, so you can pick back up without losing your place.
